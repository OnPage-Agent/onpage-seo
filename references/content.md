# Article ideas and briefs from keywords

Work from the keywords the user gives you, the site's own pages (if you can see them) and what
the business sells. Do not invent search volumes, difficulty scores or traffic estimates. If the
user asks for them, say they need keyword data (Google Keyword Planner, Search Console for
searches the site already shows up for, or a tool such as OnPage Agent).

## 1. Sort the keywords

- **Drop** searches about another company's product, reviews or complaints (unless it's an honest
  "X alternative" comparison for a direct competitor), and searches unrelated to what the site
  sells. Say which you dropped and why.
- **Group** the rest by what the searcher wants: learn (how/what/why), compare (best, vs,
  alternatives), buy or hire (pricing, near me, service, agency, tool), or find a specific site.
- **Merge** keywords that one page should answer together ("crm for small business" and "small
  business crm"); one page per intent, not per phrasing.

## 2. Match to the site

For each group, say whether an existing page already covers it (improve that page) or it needs a
new one. Buying-intent searches usually belong on service, product or pricing pages, not blog
posts.

## 3. Ideas

For each new page: working title (under 60 characters, the main keyword near the start), the
search intent, who it's for, the existing pages it should link to and be linked from, and one
line on why the site can answer it better than what's out there (first-hand experience, data,
a tool, local knowledge).

## 4. Brief (when asked)

- Title and meta description (120 to 155 characters)
- H1, then H2 sections in the order a reader needs them; answer the main question in the first
  paragraph
- Questions to answer (from the user, the site's FAQs, or real customer questions they share)
- Facts, examples or numbers the writer must supply from the business (don't make them up)
- Internal links (2 to 5) with the anchor text to use
- Suggested structured data (Article; Product or Service where it fits)

Write for people first: specific, first-hand where possible, no filler intros, no keyword
stuffing. Google's guidance: https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content
